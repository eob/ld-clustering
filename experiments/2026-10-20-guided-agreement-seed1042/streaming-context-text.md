Prior text observations for the contextual manager

These observations come from a completed earlier controlled sample, disjoint
from this stream. They inform catalog design, not a required category list.
Construct the current catalog from the current observed examples and objective.
Previous category names below describe that earlier catalog only. Observed
assignments and automated reviews can both be wrong.

One discovery-only catalog had ten broad practical subjects. Replacing its
short descriptions with a joint membership/boundary rewrite reduced matched
Sol–Decisions agreement from 820/1000 to 522/1000, and matching named IDs from
809 to 511. Both-Other counts stayed at 11. The rewrite increased description
words from 215 to 1435. Wording, length and cross-references changed together;
this does not prove which component caused the decline. Do not copy the long
rewrite or assume that more explicit prose automatically improves classification.

Observed examples and automated review:

- “what is the balance on my vacation days”: Sol chose the earlier Work group,
  Decisions Travel. The reviewer supported the leave-balance interpretation.
  Workplace context can be supported without naming an employer. Details needed
  to answer a request are not always needed to identify its practical subject.
- “that's incorrect”: Sol chose assistant interaction, Decisions Other. The
  earlier guide explicitly included conversational correction; the reviewer
  supported that membership. “i seem to have lost my phone” likewise established
  a device-location subject despite being a statement rather than an imperative.
- “how to paint walls”: Sol rejected it, Decisions chose the earlier Knowledge
  group. The reviewer supported that general instructional membership. A broad
  explanatory category should retain its stated remaining scope.
- “search information on how to apply foundation”: Sol chose Other and Decisions
  Cars. The reviewer supported neither, preferring general instruction. Cosmetic
  application is distinct from purchasing or recommending a cosmetic product.
- “who played ronald weasly”: Sol chose Entertainment, Decisions Knowledge. The
  reviewer preferred the factual interpretation but found the earlier media
  scope a plausible alternative. A facts/media boundary needs a consistent rule.
- “my visa has been damaged” could mean a payment card or a travel document.
  The reviewer treated Sol's Banking interpretation as plausible but uncertain;
  Decisions' Food choice lacked support. Preserve such uncertainty rather than
  inventing a resolving story.
- Both methods assigned a word-definition query to Knowledge and an unidentified
  reservation cancellation to Other. The reviewer supported those agreements.
  No clearly shared error was established among the thirty sampled guided-text
  agreements; this is limited automated evidence, not proof of semantic accuracy.

Short utterances deserve practical interpretation. Conversational feedback and
acknowledgments can carry their own interaction meaning. Some reviewer judgments
favored caution for fragments such as “affirmative”; that is an uncertain
interpretation, not an instruction to reject all short conversation. Use only
context supported by the item and observed scope.

Choice arrangement is another unresolved limitation. On a preselected 100-item
guided subset, cross-model agreement was 49 at K8, 61 after reversing order, and
34 with all ten choices on one page. Reversal changed 57 Decisions assignments
and four Sol assignments. It also changed page composition. These secondary
passes include stochastic variation and do not identify a pure order, page,
wording or length mechanism. Useful semantic categories and coverage remain the
goal; artificially increasing rejection is not a solution.
