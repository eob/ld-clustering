Prior photograph observations for the contextual manager

These observations come from a completed earlier controlled sample, disjoint
from this stream. They inform catalog design, not a required category list.
Construct the current catalog from current observed photographs and the stated
objective. Previous category names below describe the earlier catalog only.
Observed assignments and automated reviews can both be wrong. Photo source
titles, dataset tags and authorship are not evidence of visible identity.

An eight-category discovery-only catalog produced 96/100 matched category IDs
with short descriptions. A joint membership/boundary rewrite reduced agreement
to 81/100, and matching named IDs from 91 to 76. Both-Other counts stayed at five,
although the rejected identities changed. Description words increased from 142
to 1094. Content, length and cross-references changed together. No individual
component was causally isolated, and longer descriptions were not an improvement
in that comparison.

Observed photographs and automated review:

- A person playing a brass instrument was assigned People by Sol and Food by
  Decisions. The reviewer found the Food assignment unsupported. Similar clear
  People/Food mismatches occurred for human sport and work scenes. Nearby food,
  equipment or background objects should not automatically displace the focal
  person or action.
- An exposed coil connected to wires, clips and components was assigned
  Electronics by Sol and Other by Decisions. The reviewer supported recognizable
  electronic equipment without requiring a finished enclosure or operation.
  Its appearance does not establish manufacturing provenance or an exact device
  subtype.
- An unidentified pale granular close-up was assigned Food by Sol and Other by
  Decisions. The reviewer preferred Other because visible appearance did not
  reliably establish edibility, while treating Food as a plausible possibility.
  High resolution alone cannot establish every semantic identity.
- A courtyard with prominent vegetation and masonry was assigned Plants by Sol
  and Buildings by Decisions. The reviewer mildly preferred the integrated
  architectural scene, while accepting vegetation as a plausible emphasis.
- A masonry cooking apparatus was assigned Appliances by Sol and Structures by
  Decisions. The reviewer found both reasonable: device identity and built form
  overlap. Such boundaries need coherent scope and a practical tie rule rather
  than conflicting parent/child options.
- A natural cave was assigned Other by Sol and Buildings by Decisions. The
  reviewer supported Other: visitor lighting did not make the natural formation
  primarily a constructed environment. Missing coverage can justify rejection.
- Both methods assigned a flower-centered photograph to Plants. Both rejected a
  helmet with an attached camera; the reviewer found that shared rejection
  uncertain because protective headwear and electronics interpretations remained
  plausible. Of 81 guided-photo agreements, eighty were supported and one was
  unclear according to that reviewer. This is automated same-family assessment,
  not human semantic ground truth or proof that shared mistakes are absent.

Every image category fit on the first K8 page. Page boundaries cannot explain
those primary photo errors. On the preselected 25-photo diagnostic subset,
agreement was 21 at primary K8, 22 after reversing options and 22 on K16. K16 had
identical per-item request bodies: Decisions repeated all 25 IDs while Sol
repeated 24, so this was an extra pass rather than a pagination intervention.
These small secondary checks include stochastic variation. Seek clear visible
membership and useful coverage rather than optimizing agreement by rejection.
